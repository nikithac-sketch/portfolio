Hurrey

Hurrey is an EdTech startup building an interconnected school superapp to dismantle the "one-size-fits-all" classroom. By unifying teachers, parents, administrators, and students, their platform anchors around three pillars: Personalised Learning, Breaking Silos, and Positive Reinforcement.

I joined Hurrey as a graduation design intern with an open mandate: take their early beta module, diagnose why adoption was struggling, and transform it into an enterprise tool that educators actually loved using.

Naturally, I chose the quietest, most overlooked corner of the school: The Library.

Everything looked clean on paper... UNTIL THE 40-MINUTE LUNCH RUSH.

SERVICE SAFARI

A digital tool that felt more manual than paper

To understand why the beta was failing, I shadowed school librarians through their daily crucible: 40 minutes of recess when 200 energetic students flood the circulation desk.

01 Catalog Bloat
"I don't need a Netflix movie poster. I need to know if Copy #2 of Wings of Fire is on Shelf 4B!"

The 80% Cover Disaster

The catalog prioritized massive book cover artwork taking up 80% of screen real estate. Essential metadata — shelf classification, accession number, and live copy counts — was completely hidden. Only two rigid filters existed, with zero sorting.

02 Circulation Bottleneck
"When 30 sixth-graders are queuing before the bell rings, typing 13-digit ISBNs by hand is a nightmare."

14 Clicks & Keyboard Fatigue

Issuing a single book required opening a modal, typing an entire 13-digit ISBN without auto-complete, and selecting 7 mandatory dropdowns. One typo reset the form. Average checkout time: 4.2 minutes per student.

03 Siloed Data
"We bought 400 books at the annual fair. The software asked me to manually type every single publication date."

The Anti-Standard Island

The system ignored established library science protocols like MARC 21 and Dewey Decimal Classification. It was completely isolated from global publishers and other Hurrey superapp modules.

THE REDEFINED BRIEF
"Redesign the UI/UX of Hurrey's digital library system for school librarians — eliminating operational friction, honouring conventional library standards, and harnessing contextual AI to transform the library from a book warehouse into an active learning engine."
01

Who we're designing for

Enterprise school software cannot treat all institutions the same. Through contextual inquiry, four distinct school archetypes emerged — and two were chosen for product focus.

SELECTED · V1 LAUNCH ARCHETYPE 01

The Vanguard Academy

“Our library isn't storage. It’s an intellectual incubator for young researchers.”

High budget with progressive international curricula (IB/Cambridge). Heavily prioritizes student inquiry, digital literacy, and cross-disciplinary projects. Open to smart AI recommendations.

Tech Readiness ✓ Reading Culture ✓ High Budget ✓
SELECTED · MAXIMUM IMPACT ARCHETYPE 02

The Resilient Striver

“We have a modest budget, but our kids devour books. Every minute saved from clerical work is a minute I spend with them.”

Mid-to-low budget private institution with immense heart. A single solo librarian handles cataloging, discipline, and checkout. Desperately needs automation to survive daily chaos.

High Passion ✓ Student Focus ✓ Budget Constraints —
DEPRIORITIZED ARCHETYPE 03

The Exam Fortress

“The library is strictly a silent study hall for board exam preparation.”

Academically rigid school obsessed with standardized testing scores. The library serves as silent seating for textbooks; recreational reading and exploratory literature are actively discouraged.

Syllabus Locked ✓ Recreational Reading —
OUT OF SCOPE ARCHETYPE 04

The Dormant Archive

“The books stay locked behind glass cases so they don’t get damaged or lost.”

Severe budget starvation and bureaucratic inertia. Books are preserved as institutional property rather than shared learning assets. Digital adoption readiness is near zero.

Low Usage — Zero Digital Infra —

Why Vanguard + Striver? Designing for the Vanguard gave us the architectural headroom for advanced AI intelligence (smart reading planners, catalog analytics). Designing for the Striver ensured the interface remained lightning-fast, ultra-tolerant of manual constraints, and dead simple to navigate.

02

Deciphering 60 years of library science

Before writing a line of UI code, I had to learn how librarians have categorized human thought since 1876. Modern UX cannot ignore global bibliographic standards.

MARC 21
Machine-Readable Cataloging

The global data format invented in the 1960s that refuses to die. Ensuring full MARC 21 field compliance meant schools could export and import records across Follett, Koha, and national libraries without data loss.

ISBN
International Standard Book Number

The 13-digit global fingerprint of every publication. By integrating an open ISBN database lookup, typing an ISBN auto-populates title, author, edition, and publisher in 0.4 seconds.

DDC
Dewey Decimal Classification

From 000 (Computer Science) to 800 (Literature) and 900 (History). Adding automated DDC category mapping gives librarians instant shelf spine numbering suggestions.

ACC#
Accession Tracking

The vital distinction between Title and Physical Inventory. One book title can have 5 identical copies; accession numbers track the specific condition and location of Copy #3.

THE CUTTING ROOM FLOOR

See what didn't work

Great design is defined as much by what you ruthlessly kill as what you ship. Here are three early concepts that failed user testing and why they were cut.

REJECTED CONCEPT 01

The Netflix-Style Cover Carousel

Hypothesis: Visual carousels of book covers will make the library feel modern, playful, and student-friendly.

Librarian Reality: "I’m not watching a movie; I’m managing 4,000 physical assets. I need high-density tables, accession numbers, and rapid keyboard navigation."
REJECTED CONCEPT 02

The 18-Field Universal Modal

Hypothesis: Putting book issuance, fine collection, and return status into one giant dialog window will save clicks.

Librarian Reality: Cognitive overload. If a student asked a question midway through, the modal was closed, destroying all unsaved form state. We switched to an empty-state guided page flow.
REJECTED CONCEPT 03

Autonomous AI Purchasing Bot

Hypothesis: The AI should automatically generate purchase orders for out-of-stock titles based on request frequency.

Librarian Reality: Complete administrative rejection. School principals require human gatekeeping before budget funds are deployed. The feature was pivoted into the Recommendation Centre.

04

Unlocking Hurrey's AI superpower

Hurrey’s competitive edge was its AI engine. But instead of superficial conversational chatbots, we embedded machine intelligence where it solved unglamorous, high-friction operational pain.

📊

Predictive Restock Engine

Cross-references student search queries, unmet hold requests, and the school’s upcoming academic calendar. Predicts surges in demand 3 weeks before teachers introduce topics, giving librarians time to procure copies.

🧹

Smart Weeding Assistant

Physical library shelves have hard limits. The weeding algorithm flags dead titles with zero circulation across 24 months, recommending them for archive storage or book sales to liberate shelf space.

✨

The Library Planner

Librarians select a grade level, pedagogical theme (e.g., "Eco-fiction & Climate"), and time horizon. The system analyzes readability scores and catalog availability to generate a structured reading curriculum in 5 seconds.

🗓️

Spotlight: The Library Planner

Before this feature, building curated reading plans for English teachers took librarians an entire weekend of cross-referencing syllabi with physical shelves. With Library Planner, it takes 10 seconds — complete with age-appropriate reading difficulty scores and direct reserve links.

FOUNDATIONAL SYSTEM

Google Material Design — adapted for data density

Enterprise SaaS for education demands rock-solid consistency across desktop monitors, school administrative laptops, and portable tablets. In collaboration with Hurrey’s engineering team, we adopted Google Material Design as our baseline foundation.

However, default Material components are notoriously generous with white space. We engineered custom dense variants: tight tabular row heights, persistent sticky column headers, high-contrast status pills, and keyboard shortcuts (`Enter` to issue, `Esc` to clear).

High-Density Tables Faceted Filters Navigation Drawer Barcode Modal Status Badges Keyboard Accelerators Calendar Engine Multi-language Tokens
05

The final experience

Explore the six core production flows — built for zero friction, high operational velocity, and intelligent assistance.

Dashboard (Empty State) — Friendly onboarding cards guiding first-time librarians to import books, set loan periods, and connect student registers.
Dashboard (Operational Velocity) — Live pulse of library health: daily checkouts, overdue alerts, and high-velocity books at a single glance.
Student Verification — Instant barcode scan or student name search. Instantly renders past loan history, unreturned books, and active restrictions.
Lightning Checkout — Scan book accession code → auto-validates return due date based on grade rules → confirms in 1 click.
Instant Confirmation — Unambiguous confirmation badge with quick option to print receipt or send automated WhatsApp notification to parents.
High-Density Catalog — Tabular view with multi-faceted filtering (Genre, DDC, Author, Grade, Availability) and instant alphabetical sort.
Bibliographic Record — Complete MARC 21 metadata, shelf spine code, edition records, and real-time copy tracking across school blocks.
Smart Add (ISBN Fetch) — Type 13 digits → system queries global library repositories and populates all fields automatically.
Priority Request Inbox — Familiar Gmail-style triaging: unread hold requests highlighted, fulfilled requests archived with timestamps.
Request Actions — Review student note, reserve copy on hold, or trigger procurement review in one streamlined drawer.
Narrative Reports — Replaces confusing scatter plots with plain-language analytical summaries that librarians can paste into board presentations.
Restock Intelligence — Algorithmic purchasing suggestions ranked by student request frequency, curriculum trends, and current inventory deficits.
Circulation Policies — Granular controls for loan periods, renewal limits, fine tiers, and reminder schedules per student grade tier.
Classification Rules — Customize Dewey Decimal categories, custom accession prefixes, and campus branch allocations.

REFLECTION

What 6 months in a school library taught me

This graduation project was my first deep immersion into enterprise B2B SaaS — and it shattered my student illusions about what "good design" looks like. In school, we are taught to design for visual delight and fluid consumer animations. In an enterprise tool, delight is getting out of the user's way as fast as humanly possible.

When you watch a librarian face down 30 impatient kids during a 15-minute recess, you stop caring about trendy design dribbble shots. You care about keyboard shortcuts. You care about error recovery that doesn't wipe 8 fields when you make a typo. You care about high-density tables that fit 40 records on a budget 1080p screen.

And AI? This project proved that AI is most powerful when it is humble. Not a chat bubble interrupting your flow, but an invisible background engine that looks at an ISBN and fills out 12 fields so a human doesn’t have to.

Six months, five pilot schools, zero lost library cards, and a permanent respect for the quietest room in education. 📚✨
